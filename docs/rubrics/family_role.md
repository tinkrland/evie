# rubric: family_role (layer 2, single, core)

how much of the story is family. not whether a family exists in the
fiction, but what work the family does in the plot: is it the reason
to watch, a second engine beside the main one, a recurring thread, or
set dressing.

the owner's distinction, stated plainly: divide by whether the family
portion is relevant as primary plot versus subplot.

## how to tag

- ask: if you removed every family scene, what breaks? if the show
  collapses, family is primary. if a thread vanishes, subplot. if
  nothing changes, backdrop or none.
- family means chosen family too, but only when the show treats it as
  family (the literal family in blue bloods; not the team in ncis).
  the team is ensemble_shape's business; this axis is about kin.
- none is a real value. a show about a lone professional has none.

## values

### primary
- definition: the family is the plot. episodes are family episodes
  that happen to contain the job.
- examples: blue bloods (the sunday dinner is the show's spine and
  three generations are the cast), gilmore girls (mother and daughter
  are the premise).
- counterexample: a family that shares screen time evenly with cases.
  that is co-primary.

### co-primary
- definition: two engines running, the family and the case, and
  neither is optional.
- examples: fleabag (the family grief carries as hard as the
  romance), mare of easttown (the grandson custody fight is a primary
  plot), monk (trudy's murder is the wound the series runs on).
- counterexample: a custody storyline that appears for one arc and
  resolves. that is a subplot.

### subplot
- definition: a recurring family thread under the main action: the
  parent who trained you, the sibling who calls, the anniversary that
  returns.
- examples: psych (henry's approval and training run under every
  case).
- counterexample: a family flashback that never returns. that is
  backdrop.

### backdrop
- definition: family exists as texture: a home, a spouse, a kid who
  appears when convenient and never steers the plot.
- examples: the rookie (nolan's personal life surfaces around the
  job), izombie (family threads stay peripheral).
- counterexample: a partner whose death fuels the whole series. that
  is co-primary, even though the person is gone.

### none
- definition: no literal family thread at all.
- examples: house, white collar, slow horses. note: slow horses has
  found family in the team, but kin is absent, and that belongs to
  ensemble_shape, not here.
- counterexample: a show where the team literally functions as
  siblings and the text says so. then family_role may be co-primary
  by chosen family, and the evidence must quote the kinship framing.
